The full rescue

What you'll practise

Each step follows the order a bystander would take in a real cardiac arrest. Turn your volume up: the trainer speaks its instructions out loud.

  1. Check it's safe

    Look for danger before you approach, then check for a response.

  2. Call 999

    Put your phone on speaker so you can keep your hands free.

  3. Check breathing

    While the call connects, check whether they are breathing normally.

  4. Start CPR

    Push hard and fast in the centre of the chest, keeping time with the beat.

  5. Use the AED

    Switch it on, place the pads where shown and stand clear while it analyses.

  6. Shock and restart

    Press shock if advised, then go straight back to CPR until help takes over.

Inside the trainer

Feels like the real thing

A simulated defibrillator talks you through every stage. You can choose whether the AED advises a shock, and practise alone or with a second rescuer (30 compressions to 2 breaths).

Keep it handy

Add it to your home screen

AED Trainer works like an app. Add it once and it opens full screen with its own icon, and keeps working without an internet connection.

iPhone or iPad

  1. Open the trainer in Safari.
  2. Tap the Share button (the square with an arrow).
  3. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen, then Add.

Android

  1. Open the trainer in Chrome.
  2. Tap Install if it appears, or tap the ⋮ menu.
  3. Choose Add to Home screen or Install app.

For teachers, workplaces and community groups

Use it in a session

AED Trainer works well alongside a manikin, or even a firm cushion, so people can practise compressions while the trainer talks them through the rescue.

Sharing it

Running a session

  • Use Options to set the AED to always shock, never shock, or random.
  • Switch to two rescuers to practise 30:2 with breaths.
  • Open it once on Wi-Fi beforehand and it will work offline in the room.
  • Print Super Rescuer certificates for everyone who takes part.

About

Made by a resuscitation educator

AED Trainer was created by Kieran Toner, a registered nurse and nurse lecturer in Northern Ireland who teaches resuscitation. The aim is simple: more people who feel confident to start CPR and use a defibrillator when someone collapses.

The rescue sequence follows the public-access AED sequence described in Resuscitation Council UK guidance. AED Trainer is independent and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Resuscitation Council UK, the emergency services or any AED manufacturer.

Training use only. AED Trainer is for familiarisation and practice. It is not a medical device and is not a substitute for an accredited CPR/AED course.

In a real emergency, call 999 or 112 (or your local emergency number) and follow the call handler's instructions.
